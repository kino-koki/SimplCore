                               Where the Bits Must Live: An Input-Side
                          Information Bound, Tight on a Constructible Family,
                                    for Fixed-State Spectral Models
                                                                  Anonymous submission


Abstract
We prove that exact H-hop content retrieval by any deterministic fixed-state decoder requires B >= log2 |A(v)| bits of input-side information beyond a flat
symbol alphabet, where A(v) is the set of answers compatible with the flat projection v (Theorem A). The bound is attained on an explicit binary-chain
family with |A| = 2^m; on the content-chain task used in our experiments, reachability enumeration gives |A| <= 5 (log2|A(v)| <= 2.3219), so the bound is
loose there. The difficulty is that the bound is distribution-free and depth-free: extra layers, higher state order, and kernel retuning cannot manufacture the
missing bits within the duality-preserving LTI class, because the whole computation there is a function of the flat projection. We therefore characterize
where the bits may live: an explicit KEY/VALUE tag and an alphabet partition V -> 2V each supply one bit per ambiguous token, counted in B. A positional
format prior is not a per-token channel; it restricts the support of the instance distribution and reduces |A(v)| instead. The alphabet partition folds the
mandatory bit into the symbol id: it is not bit-free, it is annotation-free. Strongest evidence: two tight structural bounds (an order-2 state for one hop; exactly
H static-LTI layers for H hops under last-layer read-out) are attained by one Toeplitz, O(1)-state construction. In the architecture module the H6 interface is
the codebook's fixed value-to-key swap rather than a learned matrix; from random initialisation of the remaining weights, H=2 with two layers reaches
1.0000 +/- 0.0000 by step 150 on five of five seeds, while one layer stays at 0.1809 +/- 0.0219 (paired difference +0.8191, 95% CI [+0.8008, +0.8375] at
1500 steps). The design rule is to budget log2 |A(v)| bits at the input or relax exactness. We make no language-modeling, perplexity, throughput, equivalence
or superiority claim. Separately, on an exactly-defined finite consolidation process we prove that zero-order codebook registration improves retrieval
monotonically, with a predicted cover time of 129.87 rounds against a measured 130.00 over five seeds; an earlier attention-parity statement is withdrawn
because it did not reproduce (W12).

Broader Impact
Generative AI tools were used for implementation assistance and language
editing; all research questions, proofs and conclusions were specified and
verified by the human author. This disclosure is subject to the ICML 2027
author instructions, which are not yet published.
This is a theoretical and synthetic-experimental study of information limits
in fixed-state sequence models. It contains no language-model training, no
human-subject data, no deployed system and no generative capability. The
immediate practical consequence is a design rule: an input-side information
budget must be accounted for before attributing a retrieval failure to model
capacity. A possible misuse is to quote the synthetic exactness figures as
evidence of general language-model parity, or to treat the withdrawn parity
claim as a performance result; both readings are excluded explicitly in
Sections 5 and 8. The work does not introduce a new mechanism for
surveillance, data collection or large-scale deployment. Its environmental
footprint is limited to CPU-only synthetic probes.
